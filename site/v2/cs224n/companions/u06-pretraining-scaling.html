Section 1 · C01

The mask recipe

From the lessonC01 §2, §3

The encoder sees the whole sentence, so how do you train it without letting it copy the answer? Mask "sat" in "the cat sat on the mat" and predict it from both sides. The model fills the blank with both eyes open: 15 percent of tokens are masked, and the loss touches only masked positions. 15 percent of 512 is 76.8, about 77 tokens per sequence carry the MLM loss. The rest are context.

From the lessonC01 §3, §4

The recipe is 80/10/10: 80 percent of the chosen tokens become [MASK] (the task), 10 percent become a random token (robustness: do not trust the mask token), 10 percent stay unchanged (robustness: the model must still predict a visible word). From compute_u06.py: masked "sat", loss 0.487, p(true) 0.614. Pretraining losses are means of this number.

From the lessonC01 §11

The broken assumption is that the model sees [MASK] at test time too. Fine-tuning has no masks, so a model trained on 100 percent [MASK] faces a train/test skew. The 80/10/10 recipe exists because of this failure. Without it the model learns "[MASK] means predict", a train-only artifact.

Nugget 1 · From the lessonC01 §3, §11

The mask token is a train-only artifact. The 80/10/10 recipe keeps the model honest about a test set that has no masks.

Why it matters companion
Any special training token is a potential skew. Whenever you add one, ask what the test-time input looks like without it.
Transfer companion
Train/test symmetry is a design constraint, not a hope. Build the test distribution into the training recipe.

Section 2 · C02

The 6.74x signal

From the lessonC02 §2, §3

MLM trains on 15 percent of tokens. What trains on 100 percent? Autoregressive training: read and predict, every step. The AR loss is U04's factorization at scale: P(w_t | prefix) for every t. One direction, every token, no masks, no recipes. The simplicity is the point: the objective scales without special cases.

From the lessonC02 §4, §5

From compute_u06.py: n = 512, AR trains on 512 next tokens, MLM on ~77 masked ones, ratio 6.74x more training signal per pass. The 6.74x is arithmetic (512/76), not a claim about quality: AR sees more signal, MLM sees both directions. The tradeoff is signal volume vs context richness.

From the lessonC02 §11

The broken assumption is that more signal per pass means better models. Early AR models underperformed MLM on understanding benchmarks at small scale: the signal advantage shows at scale with the right data. Volume is not quality.

Nugget 2 · From the lessonC02 §3, §11

The objective shapes the model more than the architecture does. MLM buys bidirectionality with 15 percent signal, and AR buys full signal with one direction.

Why it matters companion
Choose AR for generation and for scale (the modern default). Choose MLM when bidirectional context matters more than signal volume (small understanding models).
Transfer companion
Before tuning the model, check the objective: the loss function is the strongest design decision.

Section 3 · C03

One word, many vectors

From the lessonC03 §2, §3

U02 gave "bank" one vector. What does "bank" mean in "river bank" vs "bank loan"? The transformer's output at a position blends the word with its context (U05), so the representation is word-in-context, not word-in-vocabulary. Static embeddings are the special case with no context.

From the lessonC03 §4, §5

From compute_u06.py: bank|river = (0.9, 0.2), bank|money = (0.2, 0.9), bank|river2 = (0.85, 0.25). Same-sense cosine 0.998, cross-sense 0.424. Context moves the vector more than the word identity does. The cosine gap is what disambiguation looks like geometrically.

From the lessonC03 §11

The broken assumption is that contextual always beats static. On tiny data, static embeddings with a simple model can win (fewer parameters, less overfitting). Context is capacity, and capacity needs data.

Nugget 3 · From the lessonC03 §3, §11

Context moves the vector more than the word identity does. Ambiguity dissolves when the representation includes the sentence.

Why it matters companion
Choose contextual when ambiguity matters (most tasks). Choose static for cheap features at scale.
Transfer companion
A representation is a function of its context, not a property of the token alone.

Section 4 · C04

The corpus is the curriculum

From the lessonC04 §2, §3

The web is 100 TB of mixed quality. What survives into training? The pipeline: filter, dedup, mix, decontaminate. Filtering removes boilerplate and toxicity (classifiers, heuristics). Dedup removes repeats (exact hash, near-dup MinHash). Mixing sets domain ratios. Decontamination removes test-set overlap (C08). Each step is a judgment call with a measurable effect.

From the lessonC04 §4, §5

Why dedup matters: duplicates get memorized (the model sees them k times, gradient k times), memorization wastes capacity and leaks. From compute_u06.py: 6 docs, 3 exact duplicates, 3 unique. Half the toy corpus was waste. At scale the fractions are smaller but the mechanism is identical.

From the lessonC04 §11

The broken assumption is that more data is always better. Adding low-quality or duplicated data at fixed compute hurts: it displaces good tokens. Quality per FLOP beats bytes.

Nugget 4 · From the lessonC04 §3, §11

Duplicates get gradient updates k times for k copies. Dedup is not hygiene, it is capacity management.

Why it matters companion
Choose curation always (the standard). The question is how aggressive, not whether.
Transfer companion
Every repeated input is a repeated lesson. Audit your data for echoes before blaming the model.

Illustration Hash dedup. Paste documents, one per line, and see which are exact duplicates of an earlier line.

Section 5 · C05

One budget, two dials

From the lessonC05 §2, §3

You have 1e18 FLOPs. How big a model on how much data? One budget, two dials. C = 6ND: 6 FLOPs per param per token (2 forward, 4 backward, the standard estimate). Spend on N (bigger model) or D (more data).

From the lessonC05 §4, §5

From compute_u06.py: N = 1e8 params, D = 2e9 tokens, C = 6 × 1e8 × 2e9 = 1.20e18 FLOPs, tokens per param 20.0. The 20:1 finding: at fixed C, loss is minimized near D/N = 20 (the compute-optimal ratio from the scaling literature). Double the model at fixed C: D halves to 1e9 (10:1, undertrained data). Double the data: N halves (overtrained small model).

From the lessonC05 §11

The broken assumption is that the ratio is a law of nature. It is a fit to specific data and architectures: new data mixes or architectures shift it. Treat it as a starting point, then fit your own (C06).

Nugget 5 · From the lessonC05 §3, §11

C = 6ND is the whole planning conversation in one equation. 20 tokens per param is the empirical balance point, not a law.

Why it matters companion
Choose 20:1 for training efficiency. Choose larger N when inference cost dominates the lifetime bill (the deployment argument).
Transfer companion
Every allocation problem has a balance ratio. Write the equation first, then argue.

Section 6 · C06

A ruler, not a crystal ball

From the lessonC06 §2, §3

Loss falls smoothly with size. What is the functional form, and when does it lie? Power laws fit loss vs N, D, C on a log-log line within the fitted range. They predict the next point, not the next paradigm. Extrapolation past the data, new architectures, and data exhaustion all break the fit.

From the lessonC06 §4, §5

From compute_u06.py, the illustrative form L = 10 N^{-0.05}: N = 1e8 → 3.9811, 2e8 → 3.8455, 4e8 → 3.7145, 8e8 → 3.5879. Doubling N multiplies loss by 0.9659: a 3.4 percent cut per doubling. Smooth, slow, predictable. There is no first-principles derivation. The honesty is in the residuals and the range.

From the lessonC06 §11

The broken assumption is that the law holds forever. Data exhaustion (repeating tokens breaks the D scaling), architecture changes (the a and b refit), and emergent jumps at the task level (loss is smooth, capabilities are not). The law is about loss, not about what the model can do.

Nugget 6 · From the lessonC06 §3, §11

Scaling laws are fitted lines, not laws of nature. Trust them within 10x of the data. Beyond that, run the experiment.

Why it matters companion
Choose power-law fits for planning within range. Beyond that, the residuals are the honest answer.
Transfer companion
A smooth metric can hide lumpy capability. Track both the loss curve and the task scores.

Section 7 · C07

The tax on every step

From the lessonC07 §2, §3

One GPU holds 1B params but not the batch. How do 8 GPUs train one model? Split the data, share the model. Each GPU runs its shard, computes gradients, then allreduce averages them so every GPU takes the same step. The communication is the tax: 2(p-1)/p × size per step, independent of the batch.

From the lessonC07 §4, §5

Ring allreduce: p-1 scatter-reduce rounds + p-1 allgather rounds, each moving S/p: total 2(p-1)S/p. From compute_u06.py: p = 8, S = 1 GB → 1.75 GB/step. Noise: single-sample variance 1.026, batch-32 variance 0.0299, ratio 34.3. The tax is per step, the noise cut is per batch.

From the lessonC07 §11

The broken assumption is that more GPUs mean proportionally faster. The 1.75 GB tax per step at fixed batch: small batches make communication dominate. Scale the batch with p, or the speedup stalls.

Nugget 7 · From the lessonC07 §3, §11

The allreduce tax is bounded: 2(p-1)/p of the model per step, approaching 2× as p grows. Communication is a fixed cost, not a surprise.

Why it matters companion
Choose data-parallel when the model fits one GPU. Combine with model-parallel at scale (3-D parallelism). Scale the batch with p.
Transfer companion
Price coordination separately from computation. Distributed systems bill you for agreement.

Section 8 · C08

The exam leaked

From the lessonC08 §2, §3

The test set is on the web. The training set is the web. What happens? A benchmark question appears verbatim in training. The model memorizes the answer, the benchmark measures recall, not ability. The exam leaked. Decontamination: n-gram overlap between train and test, drop or flag matches. Decide the rule before training, publish it with the model card (C10). Post-hoc excuses do not count.

From the lessonC08 §4, §5

Hand-computed toy, labeled as such: 1000 test items, 50 leaked verbatim, the model memorizes all 50 and gets 400 of the rest right. Reported: 450/1000 = 45 percent. Clean: 400/950 = 42.1 percent. The leak bought 2.9 points.

From the lessonC08 §11

The broken assumption is that big data is too big to contain the test. Benchmark text is duplicated across the web (papers, blogs, GitHub): big data contains more copies, not fewer. Scale increases the risk.

Nugget 8 · From the lessonC08 §3, §11

A benchmark on leaked data measures retrieval. Decontaminate before training and publish the rule, or the score is fraud-adjacent.

Why it matters companion
Choose decontamination as the minimum. Choose private held-out tests when the claim matters (capabilities, safety).
Transfer companion
Every evaluation needs a contamination audit. A score without provenance is a number without meaning.

Section 9 · C09

The workshop and the furniture

From the lessonC09 §2, §3

The model read the web, now it must classify medical notes with 500 labels. Why does the web help? Pretraining builds the workshop, fine-tuning builds the furniture. The lower layers learn reusable features (syntax, word sense), the task head learns the decision. Transfer works when the features overlap the task. It fails on distribution shift (the honest boundary).

From the lessonC09 §4, §5

Hand-computed toy, labeled as such: sense probe on frozen vectors, 92 percent (the 0.998/0.424 geometry from C03). Random vectors: 55 percent. The gap is the transfer, measured. The probe/fine-tune gap diagnoses it: probe close to fine-tune means the features did the work, a big gap means the task needed the body to change.

From the lessonC09 §11

The broken assumption is that pretraining always helps. Negative transfer: pretraining on the wrong domain can hurt (the body must unlearn). Check with the probe before committing to fine-tuning.

Nugget 9 · From the lessonC09 §3, §11

Probe before you fine-tune. The probe/fine-tune gap tells you whether the features did the work or the task needed the body to change.

Why it matters companion
Choose transfer as the default. Choose scratch when the domain is alien (the features do not apply).
Transfer companion
Diagnose before you invest: a cheap probe beats an expensive fine-tune that teaches you nothing.

Section 10 · C10

The passport

From the lessonC10 §2, §3

You download weights. What do you need to know before trusting them? The card is the model's passport: data provenance, training recipe, evaluation scope, and known limits, in one place. Data (what, deduped how, decontaminated against what), training (objective, C = 6ND, steps), eval (which benchmarks, which are clean), limits (languages, known failures), intended use. A model without a card is an unlabeled chemical: usable, but you own the risk.

From the lessonC10 §4, §5

Hand-written toy card, labeled as such: "toy-1e8: AR objective, N = 1e8, D = 2e9, C = 1.20e18. Data: web mix, exact-deduped, decontaminated vs bench-X (13-gram). Eval: perplexity 3.59 held-out (clean). Limits: English only, no math. Use: research." Every number traceable to this unit.

From the lessonC10 §11

The broken assumption is that the paper covers it. Papers describe the idea, cards describe the artifact (the exact data mix, the exact eval). Different documents, both needed.

Section 11 · C11

Three levels of eval

From the lessonC11 §2, §3

The loss fell to 3.59. Is the model good? Three levels. Intrinsic: held-out perplexity (did it learn the distribution). Probes: linear heads on frozen features (what is in the representations). Benchmarks: task scores (what it can do). Each level answers a different question, none substitutes for the others.

From the lessonC11 §4, §5

Hand-computed toy, labeled as such: train NLL 3.40 (perplexity 30.0), held-out NLL 3.59 (perplexity 36.2), leaked-held-out NLL 3.10 (perplexity 22.2). The leak looks like a win. It is fraud. A memorizer gets low perplexity on leaked data (C08's 2.9 points). Report all three or report nothing.

From the lessonC11 §11

The broken assumption is that low perplexity means a good model. The toy: 22.2 (leaked) beats 30.0 (train). Perplexity without provenance is a number without meaning.

Nugget 10 · From the lessonC11 §3, §11

Report all three numbers or report nothing: train, clean held-out, and the provenance check. The gap between them is the interesting part.

Why it matters companion
Choose the three levels. Choose private benchmarks when the claim is capabilities (C08).
Transfer companion
A metric without provenance is a number without meaning. Publish the contamination rule alongside the score.

Section 12 · C12

The memory problem

From the lessonC12 §2, §3

1B params need 12 GB to train (fp16: weights 2 GB, grads 2 GB, Adam m+v 8 GB) but the GPU has 40 GB. What breaks first at 100B? Three axes of splitting. Data-parallel: split the batch (C07). Tensor-parallel: split layers across GPUs. Pipeline-parallel: split layers along depth. FSDP: shard weights, grads, and optimizer states, gather on demand. The art is the combination: 3-D parallelism.

From the lessonC12 §4, §5

From compute_u06.py: 1B params = 12 GB (2 + 2 + 8). Scaled by hand, labeled as such, to 100B: 1.2 TB. With FSDP across 32 GPUs: 37.5 GB each (plus activations). FSDP: each GPU holds 1/p of each tensor. Before a layer's compute, allgather the full weights (communication 1.5x per step vs data-parallel's allreduce). The mechanism is always the same: move bytes to save memory.

From the lessonC12 §11

The broken assumption is that sharding is free. FSDP's allgather adds communication per layer. At small models it can be slower than data-parallel. Shard only what does not fit.
