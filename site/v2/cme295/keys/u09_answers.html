U09 · Answers

2026-10-06 · Instructor: Stanford CME295

U09 answer key

Answers to the lesson exercises. Do not open before attempting.

C01

  • R1. 4 per step: 8 tokens / 2 steps.
  • R2. Low confidence means likely wrong. Re-masking gives the model another chance with more context unmasked.
  • R3. When coherence dominates and hardware is serial. Autoregressive is the refined incumbent.

C02

  • R4. Reasoning models trained with GRPO on verifiable rewards, the RLVR recipe.
  • R5. Position bias, verbosity bias, self-preference. The judge’s verdicts carry its own fingerprints.
  • R6. Revisit the weak lectures. 15/21 is a failing map, the pins are wishes.

C03

  • R7. 21/24 = 0.875. Good, not complete.
  • R8. Study it from primary sources or cut it explicitly. An orphan is a decision, not an oversight.
  • R9. So the map is checkable. Dangling pointers are lies the map tells about itself.

C04

  • R10. U05->U06: RL foundations carry the reasoning unit. Study U05 first.
  • R11. Ground it: find taught material beneath it or mark the chain broken and restudy.
  • R12. The final is near them and the artifact base is thinnest there. Recency plus thin evidence means risk.

C05

  • R13. Date it: “as of Oct 2026 (baseline), L8 not yet held”. Speculation labeled is honest.
  • R14. Append the update with its date, link the old claim as superseded. Never edit history.
  • R15. Readers quote without context. Inline dates travel with the claim.

C06

  • R16. Example: “Compute pass@16 for p = 0.25.” Answer: 1 - 0.75^16 = 0.990. Three minutes, one skill.
  • R17. Group norm, pass@k, fusion, ECE, bias swap. Five mechanisms, thirteen minutes.
  • R18. So every question is fair game from the syllabus. Untraced questions are surprise quizzes.

C07

  • R19. Attention, 15 downstream. The keystone.
  • R20. The edges are wrong. A late concept cannot hold up the building, redraw the graph.
  • R21. Keystones carry the most later material. A crack there collapses the most.

C08

  • R22. AR, 8 vs 6. Maturity and quality outweigh latency at equal weights.
  • R23. Tie, 2.4 vs 2.4. The weights decide, not the matrix.
  • R24. Scores drift as the field moves. Undated cells are opinions wearing numbers.

C09

  • R25. (2, 2). Queries dot keys, one score per pair.
  • R26. Softmax normalizes each row into a distribution over positions. The weights must sum to 1 to be weights.
  • R27. Token 1 (“b”). Logits [0.33, 0.67, -0.33, -0.67], argmax index 1.

C10

  • R28. Example: “Group normalization beats batch normalization on mixed-difficulty prompts at fixed rollout budgets.” Falsifiable via the delta.
  • R29. “If the reward delta is within 2 seed-std after 3 seeds, kill it.” Numeric, pre-registered.
  • R30. Ethics review for human-data work, and the limitations section for a pilot. Waive explicitly, not silently.

C11

  • R31. Data match, latency, cost, shadow, rollback.
  • R32. It is a wish. Test the rollback or do not claim it.
  • R33. Data match first: run a production sample through the lab pipeline. The gap is usually the data.

C12

  • R34. Define, toy, derive, implement/complexity, compare, debug, critique, design.
  • R35. A crack. One “I do not know” is honest, two is a structural gap.
  • R36. Rung 3 becomes “justify why this fact matters and what breaks without it”.
