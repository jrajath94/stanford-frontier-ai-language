U04 · Fisher information and experimental design

How much does one label teach?

A label is a purchase. Fisher information prices it before you buy. This unit builds the price from the slope and curvature of the likelihood, then spends the budget where the information is: close contests, connected designs, and clean labels.

Section 1 · C01, C02, C03

The slope and the curvature

From the lessonC01 · Contract 3, 5, 6

The score is the slope of the log-likelihood. Positive slope means move right. Negative means move left. Zero slope means you are at the peak. For 7 wins in 10 trials the score is 7/p - 3/(1-p). At p = 0.5 it reads 8.0: push right. At p = 0.9 it reads -22.2: push left. At p = 0.70 it is zero: the peak from U03. The score is positive left of the MLE and negative right of it, so gradient ascent climbs to the top. Lesson figure u04_f01 plots both curves.

From the lessonC02 · Contract 3, 4, 6

Fisher information measures how fast the likelihood falls away from its peak. Sharp peak: the data speaks clearly. Flat peak: many values fit almost as well. Two definitions, one number: the variance of the score, and the negative expected second derivative. For the Bernoulli, I(p) = n/(p(1-p)). At p = 0.70 and n = 10, I = 47.62. The quadratic ll_max - 0.5 I (p - mle)^2 hugs the true curve near the peak, matching within 0.01 over p in 0.55 to 0.85. Lesson figure u04_f02 overlays them.

From the lessonC03 · Contract 3, 5, 6

Curvature is how fast the slope changes. High curvature means the peak is a needle: nearby values are much worse, so the estimate is precise. Low curvature means a plateau. The Taylor expansion turns into a Gaussian with variance 1/I, so curvature directly sets the width of the uncertainty. With I = 47.6 the asymptotic standard deviation is 1/sqrt(47.6) = 0.145, matching the Bernoulli standard error from U03. The skewed warning: 9 wins in 10 gives a lopsided curve, and the symmetric interval misleads on the low side.

Nugget 1 · From the lessonC01 · Contract 3, C03 · Contract 3

The score is the slope. Its zero is the peak, and the curvature at the zero is the precision.

Why it matters companion
Three numbers from one curve: where to go, where to stop, and how sure to be. Optimization and inference read the same geometry.
Transfer companion
Gradient ascent in logistic regression. The gradient is the score, and the lesson's push-right push-left reading is the update rule.

Section 2 · C04, C05

When the identities fail

From the lessonC04 · Contract 3, 5, 6

The score identities assume you can swap differentiation and integration. Three conditions: the support of the data must not move with the parameter, the parameter must sit in the interior of its space, and the derivatives must be well-behaved. The violation is concrete. Take x uniform on (0, theta). The support moves with theta, the swap breaks, and the Fisher formula does not apply. With n = 100 and true theta = 2, the MLE is the max of the sample, about 1.98, and the error shrinks as 1/n, not 1/sqrt(n). The lab verifies the rate on a log-log plot: slope near -1, not -0.5.

From the lessonC05 · Contract 3, 5, 6

A singular information matrix means some direction has zero information: the data cannot see it. That direction is exactly the non-identifiable one. For unanchored Bradley-Terry, shifting all scores by a constant leaves the likelihood unchanged, so the information matrix times the all-ones vector is zero. The lesson's design comparison makes it numeric. Design 1 puts all 10 labels on pair A-B: I = [[2.35, 0],[0, 0]], determinant 0, the second gap unidentified. Design 2 splits 5 and 5: I = [[1.175, 0],[0, 1.175]], determinant 1.381. Lesson figure u04_f05 draws both.

Nugget 2 · From the lessonC05 · Contract 3, 7

A singular information matrix is a blind direction. The data cannot see it, and no optimizer can fix that.

Why it matters companion
Design 1 bought ten labels and learned nothing about the second gap. The eigenvalue check costs one decomposition and would have caught it before a dollar was spent.
Transfer companion
Collinear features in regression. The design matrix equivalent of the zero row, and the same infinite-variance bill.

Nugget 3 · From the lessonC04 · Contract 3, 13

Check the conditions before quoting the rates. When the support moves with the parameter, 1/sqrt(n) is the wrong promise.

Why it matters companion
The Uniform example converges at 1/n, faster than the textbook rate. The formula is not conservative there. It is simply inapplicable.
Transfer companion
Auction models, where the bid support depends on the value parameter. The non-regular theory is the right toolbox.

Section 3 · C06, C10

How many labels

From the lessonC06 · Contract 3, 4, 6

The MLE's variance is about 1/(n I): more data and sharper peaks both shrink it. Invert the formula to size the study. For p = 0.7 the per-sample information is 1/0.21 = 4.76, so a standard error below 0.05 needs n above 1/(0.05^2 times 4.76) = 84. The lesson's series: standard error 0.145 at n = 10, 0.046 at n = 100, 0.014 at n = 1000. Lesson figure u04_f03 draws the falling curve on a log axis. The honest caveat: at n = 10 the asymptotic 0.145 is only approximate, and the true sampling distribution is skewed.

From the lessonC10 · Contract 3, 5, 6

Error falls fast at first, then slowly. The simulated mean squared error for a true gap of 1.0: 0.60 at n = 10, 0.37 at n = 20, 0.17 at n = 50, 0.07 at n = 100, 0.04 at n = 200, 0.01 at n = 500, twenty trials each. The first 50 labels buy most of the precision. The stopping rule: stop when the next label buys less than it costs. The pilot-study pattern: label 50, estimate the error curve, extrapolate, decide the full budget. Lesson figure u04_f07 plots the curve with trial spread.

Nugget 4 · From the lessonC06 · Contract 4, C10 · Contract 7

Size the study before you buy the labels. The formula turns an error target into a sample size, and the pilot turns a guess into a curve.

Why it matters companion
n = 84 for a 0.05 standard error is a three-line calculation. Collecting 500 labels first and asking later is the expensive order of operations.
Transfer companion
How many users must rate an item for a 0.05 standard error? Same formula, same three lines, different p.

Section 4 · C07, C08, C09

Which label next

From the lessonC07 · Contract 3, 5, 6

Each candidate query carries information sigma(d)(1 - sigma(d)) about its gap. Close contests carry the most. Foregone conclusions carry almost none. The lesson scores three gaps: 0 gives 0.250, 1 gives 0.197, 2 gives 0.105. Labeling the gap-2 pair teaches less than half what the gap-0 pair teaches. The mechanism: for one Bradley-Terry comparison the Fisher information for the gap is the Bernoulli variance at p = sigma(d), and it peaks at p = 0.5. Lesson figure u04_f04 draws the three bars.

From the lessonC09 · Contract 3, 5, 6

Active selection always asks the currently most informative question, then updates its beliefs, then asks again. It is greedy, myopic, and usually better than random. The lesson's arithmetic: posterior precision adds the information of each asked query. Greedy banks 0.248 per label. Random banks 0.177 on average. After 30 labels the variance is 0.119 greedy against 0.159 random, a ratio of 0.75: greedy needs about 25 percent fewer labels for the same precision. Lesson figure u04_f06 draws both falling curves. The myopia warning: two queries that are jointly informative but weak alone will never be picked by a greedy rule.

Nugget 5 · From the lessonC07 · Contract 3, C09 · Contract 3

Label the close contests. A foregone conclusion teaches almost nothing, and the information price says exactly how little.

Why it matters companion
The gap-2 pair carries 0.105 against the gap-0 pair's 0.250. Spending the budget there pays full price for less than half the teaching.
Transfer companion
Which search results should a rater judge next? The ones the model is least sure about, priced by the same Bernoulli variance.

Nugget 6 · From the lessonC08 · Contract 3, 5

A design is good if its information matrix is large. Both A- and D-optimality punish blind spots, infinitely.

Why it matters companion
The criteria turn a vague labeling plan into a number before any data exists. Design 2 wins on both, and the margin is not close.
Transfer companion
A/B test allocation. The same trace and determinant choose how to split traffic across arms.

Section 5 · C11, C12

Noise and validation

From the lessonC11 · Contract 3, 5, 6

Noise dilutes every label. The per-label information falls as the flip rate rises: relative information 1.00, 0.58, 0.31, 0.13, 0.03, 0.00 for q = 0 to 0.5. At q = 0.3 each label carries 13 percent of clean information, so about 7.7 times the labels are needed for the same precision. At q = 0.5 the information is 0: pure noise teaches nothing. The cost lesson is the point of the concept: cutting noise from 0.3 to 0.1 through protocol work beats buying five times the labels. Lesson figure u04_f08 draws the falling bars.

Nugget 7 · From the lessonC11 · Contract 3, 9

Cutting noise beats buying labels. At 30 percent flips, each label carries 13 percent of clean information.

Why it matters companion
The budget multiplier is 7.7. Protocol work that moves q from 0.3 to 0.1 is worth more than five times the labeling spend.
Transfer companion
Noisy crowd labels. Budget or clean? The information curve answers before the purchase order.

From the lessonC12 · Contract 3, 5, 6

A design makes a promise: the predicted variance. Validation checks the promise against reality. Three checks: compare achieved standard errors to planned ones, check interval coverage on held-out data, and compare the active-selection gain against the predicted gain. Any failure sends you back to replan. The lesson's worked example: planned standard error 0.046 at n = 100. If the achieved is 0.09, the information came in at one quarter the planned rate. Investigate noise or bad gap guesses. Skipping validation risks the next study inheriting a broken plan.

Nugget 8 · From the lessonC12 · Contract 5, 9

A design makes a promise. Validation checks the promise, and a miss sends you back to replan.

Why it matters companion
0.09 achieved against 0.046 planned is not bad luck. It is a quarter of the promised information rate, and the next study needs the corrected guesses.
Transfer companion
An A/B test comes in underpowered. Check planned against achieved before you rerun: the design, not the data, may be at fault.
