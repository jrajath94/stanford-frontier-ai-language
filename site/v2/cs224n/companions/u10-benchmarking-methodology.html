Section 1 · C01

No winner declared

From the lessonC01 §2, §3

Model A scores 0.78, model B scores 0.76. Did A win? A benchmark is an exam with a fixed syllabus. The toy Wilson interval for 78/100 is [0.689, 0.850]. B's 0.76 sits inside A's interval. No winner declared.

From the lessonC01 §4, §5

From compute_u10.py: 78/100 gives [0.689, 0.850], 780/1000 gives [0.753, 0.805]. Ten times the items, same point, honest claim. The score is fixed, the claim shrinks with n. A mean hides the slices (C08): two models with the same mean can differ by 0.26 on a slice.

From the lessonC01 §11

The broken assumption is that higher mean means better model. A wins the mean, B wins every slice that matters to you (C08). Read the slices before the mean.

Nugget 1 · From the lessonC01 §3, §11

If B's score sits inside A's interval, there is no winner. The interval is the claim. The point is the center.

Why it matters companion
Choose public benchmarks for comparability. Choose bespoke evals when the public syllabus misses your deployment.
Transfer companion
Never declare a win without an interval. Overlapping intervals mean the race is still on.

Section 2 · C02

The map and the territory

From the lessonC02 §2, §3

The benchmark has 50 tasks, yours is not among them. What does 0.78 mean for you? The benchmark is a map. Coverage asks whether your territory is on it. A high score on the map is not a high score on your ground.

From the lessonC02 §4, §5

Hand-set toy, labeled as such: 50 tasks, 6 capabilities, column "tool use under permission errors" empty. The benchmark's 0.78 covers 5 of 6. Report 5/6 covered, not 0.78. The mechanism is the matrix: rows are benchmark tasks, columns are capabilities your deployment needs. An empty column is an untested capability. Fill it or admit it.

From the lessonC02 §11

The broken assumption is that nearby tasks predict yours. The model aces summarization benchmarks and fails your meeting-notes task (different length, different noise). Nearby is not same.

Section 3 · C03

Calibrate the instrument

From the lessonC03 §2, §3

The judge prefers A over B. Do you believe it? A judge is a measuring instrument. Calibrate it like one: agreement with humans (kappa), confidence against reality (Brier, reliability). An uncalibrated judge is an opinion with a GPU.

From the lessonC03 §4, §5

From compute_u10.py: two judges agree on 78 of 100 items, kappa 0.551 (chance-corrected). Kappa removes the agreement that chance would give: with yes-rates 0.60 and 0.55, chance alone agrees 0.51 of the time, so 0.78 observed is kappa 0.551. The judge's confidence bins miscalibrate with Brier 0.17: stated 0.8, observed 0.67 in one bin. Overconfident.

From the lessonC03 §11

The broken assumption is that the judge is neutral. The judge prefers longer answers and the model's own style. Calibrate on style-balanced pairs.

Nugget 2 · From the lessonC03 §3, §11

Calibrate the judge before you trust its verdicts. Agreement with humans, confidence against reality.

Why it matters companion
Choose the judge for scale, with calibration. Choose humans for the final gate.
Transfer companion
Every instrument gets calibrated. A model judge is an instrument with strong opinions.

Section 4 · C04

An open-book exam graded closed-book

From the lessonC04 §2, §3

The model saw the test during training. What is the score worth? Contamination is an open-book exam graded as closed-book. The model is not smarter. The test is compromised. The inflation equals the leak share times the memorization gap.

From the lessonC04 §4, §5

From compute_u10.py: 200 items, 40 leaked at 0.95, 160 clean at 0.70. Reported: 0.20 × 0.95 + 0.80 × 0.70 = 0.75. Clean-only: 0.70. The leak is worth 0.05 of pure inflation. Quarantine the 40, rescore, report 0.70 with the quarantine noted.

From the lessonC04 §11

The broken assumption is that overlap means memorization. Common phrases overlap without the answer leaking. The probe needs the answer-bearing spans, not any n-gram.

Nugget 3 · From the lessonC04 §3, §11

Small leaks, measurable lies. The inflation is the leak share times the memorization gap: quarantine and rescore.

Why it matters companion
Choose quarantine when the train set is fixed. Choose fresh sets for high-stakes claims.
Transfer companion
Every benchmark needs a contamination audit. A score without one is a hope.

Section 5 · C05

The interval is the claim

From the lessonC05 §2, §3

How many items make a claim? Every score is a sample. The interval is the honest claim, the point is the center. Small n, wide claim. Margin 0.02 at 95 percent needs n = 2401 for p = 0.5. Your 200-item benchmark has margin about 0.07: say that.

From the lessonC05 §4, §5

From compute_u10.py: n = 2401. The mechanism: SE at p = 0.5, n = 200 is sqrt(0.25 / 200) = 0.035, margin 1.96 times that = 0.069. The n for margin m: (z / m)^2 × 0.25 = 2401 at m = 0.02.

From the lessonC05 §11

The broken assumption is that items are independent. 200 items from 10 templates: the effective n is near 10. The interval lies. Report the template count too.

Nugget 4 · From the lessonC05 §3, §11

If your benchmark has 200 items, your margin is about 0.07. Say so. The interval is the honest claim.

Why it matters companion
Choose Wilson for proportions. Choose bootstrap for exotic metrics. Always state n and the margin.
Transfer companion
A score without a sample size is a rumor. A score without a margin is a boast.

Section 6 · C06

The scorer is part of the benchmark

From the lessonC06 §2, §3

The model answers correctly but not exactly. What does exact match say? "Paris" vs "paris": exact match 0, normalized match 1. The format is a judgment, not a fact. The scorer is part of the benchmark: exact match rewards formatting obedience, multiple choice rewards the right letter, judge scoring rewards whatever the judge likes.

From the lessonC06 §4, §5

Hand-set toy, labeled as such: 100 answers, 12 differ only by case. Exact: 0.78, normalized: 0.90. The 0.12 gap is format, not knowledge. The mechanism is the pipeline: generate, normalize, compare. Each stage can flip the score. Report the normalization with the number.

From the lessonC06 §11

The broken assumption is that exact match is objective. The 0.12 case gap says otherwise. Objectivity needs the normalization stated.

Section 7 · C07

Replicate first

From the lessonC07 §2, §3

Your method beats the baseline by 0.03. Did you run the baseline right? The baseline is the control group. A win against a broken baseline is a win against nothing. Replicate first, then compare, with intervals on both sides.

From the lessonC07 §4, §5

Hand-set toy, labeled as such: the paper claims 0.78, your rerun gives 0.74 with interval [0.65, 0.82]. The claim sits inside your interval: no evidence of a difference. If the reported number falls outside your rerun's interval, something differs (version, data, bug). Debug before you innovate.

From the lessonC07 §11

The broken assumption is that the baseline in the paper is the baseline in the code. The paper may use a better prompt than the released code. Replicate the artifact, not the text.

Nugget 5 · From the lessonC07 §3, §11

Replicate the artifact, not the text. A reported win needs its own interval on both sides.

Why it matters companion
Choose replication for any claim you will defend. Choose trust for exploration only.
Transfer companion
The control group comes first. A win against nothing is nothing.

Section 8 · C08

The mean hides the slice

From the lessonC08 §2, §3

The mean is 0.78. Is the model ready? The mean is a summary, the slices are the story. A model can be excellent on average and unusable for your users. Cut by what matters: language, topic, difficulty. Delta = slice score minus overall.

From the lessonC08 §4, §5

From compute_u10.py: overall 0.78, slice A 0.52 (delta -0.26), slice B 0.86 (delta +0.08). The -0.26 is the headline, not the 0.78. Small slices need their own intervals (C05's n rule applies per slice).

From the lessonC08 §11

The broken assumption is that the mean represents the slices. The mean is a weighted lie when the weights are not your users.

Nugget 6 · From the lessonC08 §3, §11

The -0.26 is the headline, not the 0.78. Read the slices before the mean, and give small slices their own intervals.

Why it matters companion
Choose slices for deployment decisions. Choose the mean for quick comparisons.
Transfer companion
Disaggregate every summary before you act on it. The worst slice is the product.

Section 9 · C09

Name the tilt

From the lessonC09 §2, §3

The judge prefers model A. Is A better or longer? Every instrument has a tilt. Benchmarks tilt toward their items, judges tilt toward style. Name the tilt, measure it, control it. Unmeasured tilt is a confounder.

From the lessonC09 §4, §5

Hand-set toy, labeled as such: blinding the judge to length moves the win rate from 0.62 to 0.53. The 0.09 was length bias. The correction: report the blinded number. The mechanism is comparison: the win rate with and without the suspected bias factor. The difference is the bias estimate.

From the lessonC09 §11

The broken assumption is that blinding fixed it. The judge may prefer its own model's phrasing even blinded (style survives blinding). Controls are iterative.

Nugget 7 · From the lessonC09 §3, §11

Name the tilt, measure it, control it. Report the blinded number, and re-check: controls are iterative.

Why it matters companion
Choose controls for published claims. Choose raw numbers for internal iteration.
Transfer companion
Every measurement has a tilt. Find it with a control, not with confidence.

Section 10 · C10

Write the contract first

From the lessonC10 §2, §3

The model scores 0.78. Do you ship? The criterion is the contract, the metric is the measurement. Write the contract before you measure, or you will negotiate with yourself after. This blocks HARKing (hypothesizing after the results are known).

From the lessonC10 §4, §5

Hand-set toy, labeled as such: criterion "worst slice above 0.70 with n = 500 per slice". Slice A is 0.52. No ship, regardless of the mean. Another: criterion "mean above 0.75 and no slice below 0.70". Result: mean 0.78, slice A 0.52. Verdict: fail. The mean passed, the contract did not.

From the lessonC10 §11

The broken assumption is that the criterion was right. The criterion may demand 0.95 on a task humans do at 0.80. The criterion needs its own review.

Nugget 8 · From the lessonC10 §3, §11

Write the ship criterion before you measure. The mean passed, the contract did not: the contract wins.

Why it matters companion
Choose pre-registration for ship decisions. Choose exploration for research.
Transfer companion
Decide the rule before the numbers arrive. Post-hoc rules are negotiations with yourself.

Section 11 · C11

Kill it on paper

From the lessonC11 §2, §3

Three project ideas, four weeks. Which one? A project is a bet. The scorecard prices the bet: feasible, data, metric, compute, novelty. Can you do it, can you measure it, does it matter. Kill early, kill on paper, kill without shame. Below 3/5: rescope or drop.

From the lessonC11 §4, §5

Hand-set toy, labeled as such (the shared toy from compute_u15.py): idea scores 3/5. The missing two (metric defined, novelty) are exactly the rescope targets. Evidence, not vibes: "data available" fails if the dataset needs a license you do not have.

From the lessonC11 §11

The broken assumption is that the scores are honest. Check each cell with evidence before you trust the total.

Nugget 9 · From the lessonC11 §3, §11

Kill projects on paper with a scorecard. The cheapest experiment is the one you never run.

Why it matters companion
Choose the scorecard for time-boxed work. Choose curiosity for open-ended research.
Transfer companion
Price every bet before you place it. Below 3/5: rescope or drop.

Section 12 · C12

Borrowed and built

From the lessonC12 §2, §3

The project works. What did you contribute? The report has two columns: borrowed and built. Borrowed is fine, it must be labeled. Built is the contribution. A report with an empty built column is a tutorial, not a project.

From the lessonC12 §4, §5

Hand-set toy, labeled as such: borrowed: transformer, BPE, Adam. Built: the chunk-size sweep and the split-rate rule. The rule is the contribution. The mechanism is the ledger: every component gets a source or an author. The novelty bar: one falsifiable claim that was not known before.

From the lessonC12 §11

The broken assumption is that a negative result is not a contribution. The rerank sweep that found nothing is a contribution if the test was fair: negative results are data.

Nugget 10 · From the lessonC12 §3, §11

Keep the ledger from day one: borrowed in one column, built in the other. One new falsifiable claim is the bar.

Why it matters companion
Choose the ledger for anything public. The alternative is misconduct.
Transfer companion
Label what you borrowed, name what you built. Negative results count when the test was fair.
