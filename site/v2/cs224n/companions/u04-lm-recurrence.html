Unit 1 · C01

One sentence, three small bets

From the lessonC01 §2, §3

How do you assign a probability to a whole sentence? P(the, cat, sat) = P(the) × P(cat | the) × P(sat | the, cat). Three small predictions compose into one sentence score, and no independence is assumed. The chain rule turns a joint distribution into a sequence of next-word bets. Training places all bets at once, because the full sentence is known. Generation places them one at a time, each depending on the last. This asymmetry, parallel training versus serial generation, shapes every LM system.

From the lessonC01 §5, §6

The chain rule is exact: P(A, B) = P(A) P(B | A), applied n − 1 times. The log turns the product into a sum, so the total loss is a sum of per-position losses. Toy numbers, hand-computed from the U01 script counts and labeled as such: P(the) = 11/35 = 0.314, P(cat | the) = 0.24 (smoothed), P(sat | cat) = 0.158 (smoothed). Log-likelihood of “the cat sat” = log 0.314 + log 0.24 + log 0.158 = −1.158 − 1.427 − 1.845 = −4.430. Perplexity = exp(4.430/3) = 4.38.

1 badbet at one position costs the same · From the lesson C01 §3, §11
Nugget 1 · From the lesson C01 §3, §11
The lesson

Training is parallel. Generation is serial. The same factorization serves both, and the mismatch between them is where the bugs live.

Why it matters. Every position contributes equally to the loss, and no position is special. The log makes the product a sum, so one bad bet at one position costs the same as one bad bet anywhere else.

Transfer. Choose autoregressive when the task generates text left to right. Choose bidirectional when the task reads whole inputs, as in classification. Train left-to-right, then ask for the middle word: the model cannot use the right side, because the factorization fixes the information flow.

Unit 2 · C02

Training wheels that come off

From the lessonC02 §2, §3

Training feeds the model perfect prefixes. What happens when the perfection stops? At step t = 2 of “the cat sat ...”, training feeds “sat” (gold). Free running feeds the model’s own prediction “on”. The next state differs. Teacher forcing is like training wheels: the model always rides on the true path, so it never learns to recover from its own errors. At test time the wheels come off. Exposure bias is the resulting drift: small errors compound because the model visits states it never saw in training.

From the lessonC02 §5, §6

The loss only ever evaluates P(w_{t+1} | gold prefix). The test distribution is P(w_{t+1} | model prefix). These are different distributions, and minimizing the first does not minimize the second. The gap grows with sequence length: each step’s error shifts the state, and the shift accumulates. From compute_u04.py: at t = 2, forced input “sat” versus free input “on” (the model’s argmax). KL(forced || free) = 2.9543 nats. The two next-word distributions barely overlap. One wrong word, one shifted state, one large KL.

2Nugget 2 · From the lesson C02 §3, §11
Nugget 2 · From the lesson C02 §3, §11
The lesson

Training loss measures forced performance. Generation needs free-run evaluation. The toy RNN has a training perplexity of 1.326 and a one-step free-run KL of 2.95 nats.

Why it matters. Low training loss does not mean good generation. The model that never saw its own mistakes cannot recover from them.

Transfer. Choose pure teacher forcing for stable, fast training (the default). Choose scheduled sampling when long free-run quality matters and you can afford the instability. Choose RL fine-tuning when the task has a reward for whole sequences.

Unit 3 · C03

Perplexity 1.326: excellent and almost meaningless

From the lessonC03 §2, §3

The RNN reports loss 0.2824. Is that good? Perplexity = exp(0.2824) = 1.326. On the training sentences, the model is nearly certain. The number is excellent and almost meaningless: it is the perplexity measured on the training data, six sentences. Perplexity is the size of the model’s confusion: 1.326 means “as confused as choosing among 1.3 equally likely words.” A model that memorizes training text gets perplexity near 1.

From the lessonC03 §6

From compute_u04.py: train loss 0.2824, perplexity 1.326. Compare the U01 bigram: 5.137 on “the cat sat”. The RNN beats the bigram on its training data, as expected: it has far more capacity and the same six sentences to memorize.

Perplexity is a filter, not a verdict. A model that memorizes six sentences reaches 1.326 and knows nothing about the seventh.

Unit 4 · C04

The state is a lossy compressor

From the lessonC04 §2, §3

How does a fixed-size network read an unbounded sentence? h_t = tanh(W_x x_t + W_h h_{t−1} + b). The state h_t is a fixed d-dim vector that must summarize the entire prefix. Everything the model knows about the past lives in those d numbers. The state is a running summary. Each step folds the new word in and squeezes the result through tanh. The squeeze keeps values bounded but also crushes information: d numbers cannot hold n words losslessly.

From the lessonC04 §5, §6

Unrolling: h_1 = f(x_1, h_0), h_2 = f(x_2, h_1), and so on. The network over time is a deep feedforward net with tied weights. Toy, hand-unrolled and labeled as such: d = 2, W_x = I, W_h = 0.5 I, b = 0, x_1 = (1, 0), x_2 = (0, 1), h_0 = (0, 0). h_1 = tanh((1,0)) = (0.762, 0). h_2 = tanh((0,1) + 0.5(0.7616, 0)) = tanh((0.3808, 1)) = (0.363, 0.762). The state carries a decayed trace of x_1 into step 2.

Nugget 4 · From the lesson C04 §3, §11
The lesson

The state is a lossy compressor, and its capacity is the model’s memory. The tying is the point: one set of weights works for any length.

Why it matters. The decayed trace (0.363 in h_2’s first coordinate) is the memory. It fades by the recurrent weight every step, which is why the state cannot hold 100 words.

Transfer. Choose RNN when the sequence is processed online and the state is a natural fit, as in streaming. Choose attention when training speed and long-range access matter more.

Unit 5 · C05

BPTT: one matrix, ten contributions

From the lessonC05 §2, §3

The loss at step 10 depends on W_h through all 10 steps. How does the gradient reach step 1? dL/dW_h = sum over t = 1..10 of dL_t/dW_h, where each term flows back through the unrolled chain from t to the start. Every step contributes. Early steps contribute through long chains. Unroll the RNN into a 10-layer feedforward net with tied weights, then run ordinary backprop. The twist: the gradient for W_h is the sum of the per-layer gradients, because W_h appears in every layer. One matrix, ten contributions.

From the lessonC05 §6

From compute_u04.py: the training loop implements exactly this double loop (reversed time inner, dh_next carried). After 300 epochs the loss falls 2.8011 → 0.2824 and next-word accuracy rises 0.034 → 0.862. The summed gradients moved all matrices in a useful direction. The script’s inner loop is BPTT, not an approximation.

From the lessonC05 §11

The broken assumption is that truncation only affects speed. k = 5 on a task needing 20-step memory: the gradient for the relevant dependency is cut, so the model cannot learn it no matter how long it trains. Truncation is a memory limit on what the model can learn. State it up front.

Unit6

Unit 6 · C06

The product decides, exponentially

From the lessonC06 §2, §3

The gradient for step 1 passes through 19 matrix products to reach the loss at step 20. What is its size? Linear RNN, dh_t/dh_{t−1} = ρ I. Then dh_20/dh_0 has norm ρ^20. ρ = 0.8 → 0.0115 (vanished). ρ = 1.2 → 38.34 (exploded). The product decides, exponentially. Each time step multiplies the gradient by a Jacobian. Twenty multiplications by 0.8 give about 0.01: the early steps get no learning signal. Twenty multiplications by 1.2 give about 38: the updates explode. Only Jacobians with norm near 1.0 let gradients travel.

From the lessonC06 §6

From compute_u04.py: ρ = 0.8 gives norms 0.8, 0.3277, 0.1074, 0.0115 at T = 1, 5, 10, 20. ρ = 1.2 gives 1.2, 2.4883, 6.1917, 38.3376. The boundary ρ = 1 is a knife edge. Training lives near it and falls off both sides.

5Nugget 6 · From the lesson C06 §3, §11
Nugget 6 · From the lesson C06 §3, §11
The lesson

The product decides, exponentially. At ρ = 0.8 and T = 100, the gradient is 2e-10. No optimizer recovers a signal that small. The information is gone, not just quiet.

Why it matters. tanh′ ≤ 1 helps, but it cannot fix a bad W_h. More steps of BPTT cannot fix a vanishing product either. The architecture must change.

Transfer. Choose the fix by the symptom: nan → clip (C08), flat long-range learning → gates (C07). Diagnose first, then treat.

Unit 7 · C07

Gates protect the gradient

From the lessonC07 §2, §3

The product of Jacobians vanishes. Can the architecture protect the gradient? The LSTM cell state: c_t = f_t × c_{t−1} + i_t × g_t. When the forget gate f_t = 1, dc_t/dc_{t−1} = 1: the gradient flows unchanged. The gate learns when to protect and when to forget. Three gates, three jobs. Forget: what to erase from memory. Input: what to write. Output: what to reveal. The cell state is the protected conveyor belt. The gates are the loaders and unloaders. GRU merges the belt and the output (two gates instead of three): cheaper, usually as good.

From the lessonC07 §5, §6

Toy, hand-computed and labeled as such: c_0 = 2.0, forget gates (1, 1, 1, 0.1) over 4 steps, input term 0. c_4 = 2.0 × 1 × 1 × 1 × 0.1 = 0.2. Gradient dc_4/dc_0 = 0.1: the gate chose to forget at step 4, and the gradient reflects the choice. With f = 1 everywhere, the gradient would be exactly 1.0 across any number of steps.

3 loseattention at scale U05 · From the lesson C07 §3, §11
Nugget 5 · From the lesson C07 §3, §11
The lesson

The gate chose to forget, and the gradient reflects the choice. When f = 1 everywhere, the gradient is exactly 1.0 across any number of steps.

Why it matters. Gates do not solve vanishing gradients completely. The gates themselves are learned through the same products, and a badly initialized forget gate (near 0) wipes memory every step. The forget-gate bias trick (init at 1.0) exists because of this failure.

Transfer. Choose LSTM when the task needs fine memory control and compute allows. Choose GRU for a cheaper close substitute. Choose vanilla RNN almost never: it is the teaching tool, not the production tool. All three lose to attention at scale (U05).

Unit 8 · C08

Clipping is a speed limit

From the lessonC08 §2, §3

The gradient norm is 29.0 and the loss just went nan. What now? Clip at 1.0: g ← g × (1.0/29.025). New norm exactly 1.0, direction identical. The step is smaller, the compass is the same. Clipping is a speed limit, not a rerouting. It caps the step size when the gradient explodes, so one bad batch cannot throw the parameters into nan territory. It does not fix the cause (the product in C06). It contains the symptom.

From the lessonC08 §6

From compute_u04.py: random gradient norm 29.025, cap 1.0 → norm 1.000, direction kept. The training loop clips at 5.0 every step. Without it, the radius-2.0 run nans.

7Nugget 7 · From the lesson C08 §3, §11
Nugget 7 · From the lesson C08 §3, §11
The lesson

Clipping contains explosions. It cannot create signal. For vanishing gradients (norm 2e-10), clipping does nothing.

Why it matters. Choose global norm clipping as the default: the direction is kept. Per-value clipping changes direction and is cruder. Never rely on clipping alone for a fundamentally unstable architecture.

Transfer. Diagnose first (which regime?), then treat. A speed limit helps the car that is too fast. It does nothing for the car with no fuel.

Unit 9 · C09

Padding: the mask is non-negotiable

From the lessonC09 §2, §3

The batch has lengths 6, 5, 4, and the tensor needs one shape. What fills the gaps, and what stops the filler from training the model? Pad id 0, mask m_t = 1 for real tokens, 0 for pads. Loss = sum(m_t L_t) / sum(m_t). Padding is a scaffold: it makes the batch rectangular, then the mask marks the scaffolding so the loss ignores it. The model still processes pad tokens (wasted compute). The mask only protects the loss.

From the lessonC09 §6

Toy, hand-computed and labeled as such: lengths (3, 2), n_max = 3. Losses [[0.5, 0.4, 0.3], [0.6, 0.2, 9.9]], where 9.9 is the pad position’s loss. Unmasked mean = 11.9/6 = 1.983. Masked mean = (0.5+0.4+0.3+0.6+0.2)/5 = 0.4. The pad loss (9.9) dominates the unmasked number. The mask removes it.

From the lessonC09 §11

The broken assumption is that the model ignores padding by itself. Forgetting the mask trains the model to predict padding: the gradient pushes the model to predict pad_id everywhere, because it is the most frequent “token” in a padded batch. The model learns the scaffolding, not the language.

Illustration Mask toggle. The numbers are the lesson’s toy. Flip the mask and watch the scaffolding vote.

8Nugget 8 · From the lesson C09 §3, §11
Nugget 8 · From the lesson C09 §3, §11
The lesson

The mask is non-negotiable in every batched variant. Build it from lengths every time.

Why it matters. The unmasked loss is dominated by the easiest “token”: always predict pad. The bug is silent. No shape error, wrong training.

Transfer. Choose padding + mask for the standard tradeoff. Choose bucketing when length variance is high. The mask stays in all variants.

Unit 10 · C10

Reset the state between sequences

From the lessonC10 §2, §3

After “the cat sat”, the state holds the sentence. The next batch starts “a dog barked”. Does the state carry over? Yes by default (the tensor persists), and that is a bug: the new sentence inherits the old sentence’s summary. Reset h_0 = 0 between sequences. The state is short-term memory with no expiration date. Training resets it per sequence, because each example is independent.

From the lessonC10 §6

Toy, hand-computed and labeled as such: two sequences, d = 2. Reset: h_0 = (0, 0) for both. Carry by mistake: sequence 2 starts from sequence 1’s final state (0.363, 0.762) (from C04’s unroll), and its first prediction shifts toward “sat”-continuations. The bug is silent: no shape error, wrong conditioning. In real code it appears as mysteriously order-dependent validation scores: shuffle the validation set and the score changes, because the state leaked across sequences.

9Nugget 9 · From the lesson C10 §3, §11
Nugget 9 · From the lesson C10 §3, §11
The lesson

The state does not reset itself. Carry it across a batch boundary and the new sequence inherits the old summary, silently.

Why it matters. Stateful training across chunks is the deliberate exception: carry values, but detach the graph, or BPTT spans batches and memory blows up.

Transfer. At inference, carrying the state across a conversation is the correct behavior: it is the memory. In training, reset. The same tensor, opposite rules.

Unit 11 · C11

Decoding: a risk knob

From the lessonC11 §2, §3

The model outputs probabilities. How do you pick words? Scores (2.0, 1.0, 0.5, 0.1) → probs (0.587, 0.216, 0.131, 0.079) at T = 1. Greedy picks index 0 always. T = 0.5 sharpens: top prob 0.828. T = 2.0 flattens: top prob 0.406. Decoding is a risk knob. Greedy: no risk, often dull and repetitive. Temperature scales the logits before softmax: low T sharpens (confident), high T flattens (wild). Top-k and top-p cut the tail, then sample. The knob trades coherence for variety. There is no universally right setting.

From the lessonC11 §5, §6

As T → 0, softmax(z/T) → one-hot on the argmax (greedy). As T → infinity, it → uniform. Dividing by T scales the gaps, and exp amplifies the scaled gaps. From compute_u04.py: greedy → index 0. T = 0.5: top prob 0.828. T = 2.0: top prob 0.406. Same logits, three behaviors.

From the lessonC11 §10, §11

The broken assumption is that higher temperature means more creative means better. At T = 2.0 on the toy, the top prob is 0.406 and the tail dominates: the output is incoherent. Creativity without control is noise. Choose greedy for speed and determinism, sampling for variety, beam for quality under a sequence-level score. None fixes a bad model.

Unit 12 · C12

Lengths it did not see

From the lessonC12 §2, §3

Trained on length-6 sentences, what happens on length-20? The state is d = 16 numbers. A length-20 sentence must squeeze through the same 16 numbers. Early words are crushed (C04). RNNs generalize along the training distribution’s support: new word orders, seen lengths. They fail off-support: much longer sequences, novel compositions. The fixed state is a hard ceiling. Attention (U05) removes it by giving every position direct access to every other.

From the lessonC12 §6

The lesson states the mechanism as a designed experiment, not a result: the trained RNN scores 0.862 next-word accuracy on length-6 training sentences, and a drop is expected on length-12 continuations. The unit’s arc ends here: U05 is the answer to this concept’s problem.

6 memorizedsentences means the model learned the sentences · From the lesson C12 §3, §11
Nugget 10 · From the lesson C12 §3, §11
The lesson

Generalization is measured off-support, never on it. 0.862 on six memorized sentences means the model learned the sentences, not the language.

Why it matters. The failure is predictable from the unit’s mechanisms: lossy compression (C04) plus vanishing products (C06). That predictability is the point of the synthesis.

Transfer. Choose RNNs where they fit: short sequences, streaming. Choose attention when length generalization matters. The ceiling is architectural, not a tuning problem.
