U10 · Research projects and oral mastery

Run it as if someone audits the run

Nine units of machinery, one research project. Write the plan before the numbers exist. Read the baseline literature. State a falsifiable hypothesis. Run the baselines, report the uncertainty, reproduce the run, and defend it out loud. This unit is the course's research practice: the capstone replication of dueling Thompson sampling, including its failed hypothesis.

Section 1 · C01, C02, C03

Plan before you run

From the lessonC01 · Contract 3, 5, 6

A pre-analysis plan names the hypotheses, the metric, the sample, the success rule, and the failure rule before any number exists. It turns success into a checkable claim. The lesson's capstone A plan: H1, dueling TS beats uniform. H2, leader-focused TS beats vanilla dueling TS. Metric: mean cumulative strong regret. T = 200, 20 seeds. Success: lower mean with non-overlapping 95 percent CIs. Failure: overlap or reversal. The plan is timestamped before the data run. Lesson figure u10_f01 draws the five elements.

From the lessonC02 · Contract 3, 5

Read the baseline literature before proposing. The reading contract has four cells: the problem statement, the theorem, the proof sketch, the experimental setup. The lesson's worked example is the pairwise-comparison bandit: learn the best arm from pairwise comparisons, Thompson sampling achieves low regret empirically, Bradley-Terry simulator, 4 arms. Your contribution is measured against the strongest baseline, not the weakest. Read the proof, not just the abstract.

From the lessonC03 · Contract 3, 5, 6

A hypothesis names the intervention, the outcome, the direction, and the condition. A falsifiable hypothesis names the intervention, the outcome, the direction, and the condition. For example: the leader-focused variant yields lower mean strong regret than vanilla TS on the 4-arm BT bandit. The vague version, that a better acquisition helps, is not falsifiable. The lesson's H2 failed: leader TS mean 8.36 against TS 3.11. The failure is data. One sharp hypothesis per experiment beats five vague ones.

Nugget 1 · From the lessonC01 · Contract 3, 7

Freeze the plan before the numbers exist. Hypotheses, metric, sample, success rule, failure rule: five elements, timestamped.

Why it matters companion
The failure rule is written before the numbers. That is what makes success checkable instead of autobiographical.
Transfer companion
An A/B test. The success metric and the sample size go in the doc before the test runs, or the test is theater.

Nugget 2 · From the lessonC02 · Contract 3, 7

Read the proof, not just the abstract. Your contribution is measured against the strongest baseline.

Why it matters companion
The four-cell contract forces specifics: numbers, not adjectives. A misread baseline voids the comparison.
Transfer companion
A drug trial. The baseline is the standard of care at its best dose, not a straw man.

Section 2 · C05, C06, C11

The baselines and their uncertainty

From the lessonC05 · Contract 3, 5, 6

The capstone A replication ran the baseline ladder on the 4-arm Bradley-Terry dueling bandit. Uniform dueling: mean strong regret 17.24, CI [16.56, 17.92]. Dueling TS: 3.11, CI [2.23, 3.99]. Leader-focused TS: 8.36, CI [6.86, 9.87]. The verdict rule: lower mean plus non-overlapping 95 percent CIs. H1 supported: TS beats uniform by 14.13 with no CI overlap. H2 not supported: leader TS is worse than TS by 5.25. Lesson figure u10_f02 draws the bars with CIs.

From the lessonC06 · Contract 3, 5, 6

Report what the numbers do not know. CI = mean ± 1.96 × SD / √n, n = 20 seeds. Uniform: SD 1.55, tight. Dueling TS: mean 3.11, SD 2.01, CI [2.23, 3.99]. Leader TS: SD 3.43, wide. The SD is information, not noise to hide: the extension is unstable. State what varies (seeds) and what does not (the environment).

From the lessonC11 · Contract 3, 5, 6

A negative result is a result. The report has four parts: the hypothesis, the numbers, the diagnosis, what it rules out. H2 not supported: leader TS 8.36 against dueling TS 3.11, CIs [6.86, 9.87] against [2.23, 3.99]. Diagnosis: forcing duels against the least-tried challenger spends rounds on weak arms, and challenges cost strong regret. Ruled out: the claim that any leader focus helps. Lesson figure u10_f03 draws the failed comparison.

Nugget 3 · From the lessonC05 · Contract 3, 7

H1 supported, H2 not. TS beats uniform by 14.13 with no CI overlap. The extension adds 5.25 of regret.

Why it matters companion
The verdict rule is mechanical: lower mean plus non-overlapping CIs. No eyeballing curves, no p-values without effect sizes.
Transfer companion
A clinical trial readout. The treatment wins by a margin the CIs do not swallow, or it does not win.

Nugget 4 · From the lessonC06 · Contract 3, 7

Leader SD 3.43 against uniform 1.55. The SD is information: the extension is unstable.

Why it matters companion
A mean without an SD hides the instability. The wide SD is the honest part of the leader-TS story.
Transfer companion
A poll margin of error. The headline number without the ± is not a number, it is an advertisement.

Nugget 5 · From the lessonC11 · Contract 3, 7

H2 failed: 8.36 against 3.11. A negative result is a result, reported with numbers and a diagnosis.

Why it matters companion
Burying the failure is publication bias. The four-part report, hypothesis, numbers, diagnosis, ruled-out claim, is the honest alternative.
Transfer companion
A failed drug trial. The trial still taught something: the mechanism does not work that way.

Section 3 · C04, C07, C08, C09

The honest run

From the lessonC04 · Contract 3, 5, 6

Preference data is opinion data: it reveals the rater. The consent contract names purpose, voluntariness, withdrawal terms, data-use limits, and retention. The lesson's hypothetical rater pool: 25 synthetic raters, 2000 prompts × 3 pairs, a funnel from 6000 to 5664 labeled pairs. Withdrawal within 30 days, research-only use, 2-year retention: every term labeled HYPOTHETICAL in the lesson. The failure case is a promise of easy withdrawal with no removal mechanism. Lesson figure u10_f04 draws the funnel.

From the lessonC07 · Contract 3, 5, 6

Reproducible code: fixed seeds, pinned environment, committed scripts, outputs written to files. The check is the two-run diff: run twice, diff the outputs. Capstone A passed with an empty diff: identical JSON across runs. The failure case is the silent dependency update: NumPy changes a sampler, the JSON differs, and nobody recorded the version. One command reproduces the numbers, or the experiment is unverifiable.

From the lessonC08 · Contract 3, 5

A contribution statement names the delta over the strongest baseline: baseline, intervention, evidence, scope. The lesson's worked example reports a replication of dueling TS against uniform on a 4-arm BT bandit, falsifying the leader-focus extension at 8.36 against 3.11. The scope names what is not claimed: 4-arm BT simulation, not humans. A contribution without a scope is a claim without a boundary.

From the lessonC09 · Contract 3, 5

Integrity, reflection, impact: report the negative result, state the limits, name who is affected. The lesson's worked reflection: integrity (the H2 failure reported with numbers), limits (simulated BT, 4 arms, no humans), impact (better elicitation helps assistants. The same methods optimize manipulation). The lesson's audit figure u10_f05 gates the capstone: agreement pass, position pass, drift fail, size pass, decision BLOCK. A failed gate blocks. That is what gates are for.

Nugget 6 · From the lessonC07 · Contract 3, 7

Run twice, diff the outputs. Capstone A passed with an empty diff: identical JSON.

Why it matters companion
A run that works on one machine is unverifiable. The two-run check is the minimum viable reproducibility, and it fits in five commands.
Transfer companion
A clinical analysis pipeline. The regulator re-runs it. The diff must be empty.

Nugget 7 · From the lessonC08 · Contract 3, 7

Name the scope: 4-arm BT simulation, not humans. A contribution without a scope is a claim without a boundary.

Why it matters companion
The scope is what keeps 8.36 vs 3.11 from becoming a claim that the method beats Thompson sampling in general. It beats it on the simulator, nowhere else.
Transfer companion
A patent claim. The claims section is the scope. Everything outside it is not yours.

Section 4 · C10, C12

Defend it out loud

From the lessonC10 · Contract 3, 5, 6

Oral mastery is tested three ways: the quiz (timed, written), the ladder drill (one claim, eight follow-ups), the demo (one screen, one claim, live run, the decision). The eight rungs: define, toy, derive, implement and complexity, compare, debug, critique, design. No notes after rung 2. The lesson's worked ladder climbs the claim that H1 held: define strong regret, toy the 4-arm bandit, derive the verdict rule, implement the CI code, compare against uniform, debug unpaired seeds, critique the simulated BT, design the human replication. Lesson figures u10_f06 and u10_f07 draw the ladder and the demo checklist.

From the lessonC12 · Contract 3, 5

The field's open gaps, each a project a student could own: open preference datasets with full provenance, open reward-model eval rigs with subgroup slicing, replication code for published acquisition methods, audit tooling for annotation pipelines. A gap statement names what is absent, why it matters, and what "done" looks like. The lesson's figure u10_f08 maps the ten units with the gaps on top.

Nugget 8 · From the lessonC10 · Contract 3, 7

Eight rungs, no notes after rung 2: define, toy, derive, implement, compare, debug, critique, design.

Why it matters companion
The drill finds the rung you cannot climb. A skipped derivation is a missing rung, and follow-ups will find it.
Transfer companion
A thesis defense. The committee climbs the ladder with you. There is no rung 9.
