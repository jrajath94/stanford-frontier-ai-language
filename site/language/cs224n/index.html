CS224N: NLP with Deep Learning

Instructor: Chris Manning

How deep learning learned language. From word vectors to agents, this is the course that defined NLP’s deep learning era, now updated for the LLM age.

CS224N’s early lessons are its unique contribution: word vectors, dependency parsing, RNNs, and the attention story from the NLP side. Its later lessons overlap with CS336, so those are built as bridges that link back rather than rewrite.

Lessons

Part I: Words and sequences

  1. Introduction and word vectors — why words as vectors, the distributional hypothesis
  2. Word vectors and language models — word2vec, GloVe, evaluation
  3. Neural networks for NLP — backprop refresher, applied to language
  4. Dependency parsing — syntactic structure, transition-based parsing

Part II: Sequence models

  1. Recurrent neural networks — RNNs, LSTMs, vanishing gradients
  2. Seq2seq and attention — machine translation, the attention breakthrough
  3. Attention and LLMs — attention history, the road to LLMs

Part III: Transformers and pretraining (bridges to CS336)

  1. Self-attention and transformers — the architecture from the NLP side
  2. Pretraining — BERT, GPT, what pretraining learns
  3. Post-training — prompting, RLHF, instruction tuning

Part IV: Evaluation and efficiency (bridges)

  1. Benchmarking — how NLP evaluates models
  2. Efficient training — the systems side of NLP

Part V: Frontiers

  1. Brain-computer interfaces — language and the brain
  2. Reasoning and agents — chain-of-thought, LM agents
  3. After DPO — Nathan Lambert on post-training beyond DPO

Interview labs

  • Lab 1: Word2vec from scratch — implement skip-gram with negative sampling
  • Lab 2: Attention from scratch — single-head attention in NumPy
